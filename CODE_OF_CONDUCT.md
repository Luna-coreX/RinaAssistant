# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the person on the other side is trying to help and has
less context than you do.

## Where this applies

Issues, pull requests, discussions, and anywhere else this project is being
worked on or talked about. It applies to everyone taking part, including the
maintainer.

## What is expected

- **Say what is wrong, not who is wrong.** "This drops the last frame when
  the buffer is full" is a report. "This is sloppy" is not.
- **Take a correction as information.** Being wrong about something is the
  ordinary cost of working on it.
- **Leave room for people who know less than you.** A first pull request
  from somebody who has never opened a WPF project is worth more to this
  project than a perfect one from somebody who has, and treating it as an
  imposition is the fastest way to stop getting either.
- **Respect a "no".** A change can be declined because it does not fit the
  plan, and the plan is written down in [`docs/ROADMAP.md`](docs/ROADMAP.md)
  so that "no" can be argued with on the merits rather than taken
  personally.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Demeaning comments about who
somebody is — their nationality, sex, gender, orientation, disability,
appearance, age, religion, or level of experience. Publishing somebody's
private information. Sexual attention or imagery. Sustained disruption of a
discussion.

None of this needs a definition of intent attached. The question is whether
somebody reasonable would be driven away, not whether it was meant as a
joke.

## Reporting

Write to the maintainer privately: **lunadevosx.core@gmail.com**. For a
security vulnerability use the process in [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md)
instead — it is not the same thing and does not go here.

A report will be read and kept confidential. You will get an answer, though
possibly not quickly: this is a personal project, not a company with a team
on call. Say so plainly if a situation is urgent.

## What happens then

The maintainer decides, and the range runs from a private word, through
editing or removing a comment, to blocking an account. What was decided is
explained to the person who reported it.

**The maintainer is not exempt.** If the problem is the maintainer's own
behaviour, there is no higher authority in this repository to appeal to —
which is worth saying rather than pretending otherwise. What you do have:
the project is Apache-2.0 and the history is public, so anyone may take the
code and continue without permission.
